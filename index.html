About

I am a PhD candidate in the School of Computer Science at the University of Sydney. My research investigates how statistical dependence affects privacy leakage in multidimensional and temporally evolving data, particularly under Local Differential Privacy (LDP).

I develop information-theoretic measures, analytical bounds, and optimization-based mechanisms that improve the privacyâ€“utility trade-off. I was also a Visiting Research Scholar at the University of Maryland, College Park, where I worked on information-theoretic approaches to privacy-aware sharing of correlated data. Before beginning my doctoral research, I worked as a software engineer on the Ballerina team.

Research Interests

Local Differential Privacy

Designing mechanisms that protect user data before it leaves the device while retaining sufficient information for statistical analysis and learning.

Privacy Under Statistical Dependence

Quantifying the additional disclosure caused by dependencies among attributes and across repeated observations of time-varying data.

Information-Theoretic Mechanism Design

Using mutual information, conditional mutual information, divergence measures, and optimization to design principled privacyâ€“utility trade-offs.

Current Projects

  • Coordinated perturbation for controlled disclosure in multidimensional LDP
  • Theoretical and empirical analysis of correlation-induced privacy leakage
  • Stateful LDP mechanisms for repeated release of evolving numerical data
